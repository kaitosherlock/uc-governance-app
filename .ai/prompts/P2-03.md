## The impact preview, and the line you must not cross

Identify assets **within the caller's visible scope** that a policy could affect, and split the
result in two:

- `impact.known` — assets you actually enumerated and evaluated the predicate against.
- `impact.unknown` — everything you could not: objects outside the caller's visible scope, group
  membership the application cannot read, principals it cannot resolve, predicates whose semantics
  it does not fully model.

`impact.unknown` is never hidden, never collapsed, and never left empty for the sake of looking
complete.

Every impact result must be labelled, in the payload itself and not only in the UI, as **not
evaluated by Databricks**. The application computes an approximation from metadata it can see. It is
not the policy engine, and a user must not be able to mistake the two. Do not name a field or a
message in a way that implies otherwise — no `effective_access`, no `simulated_result`, no
`will_apply`.

Do not claim policy execution testing anywhere. That claim is only permissible when something
actually ran on suitable compute with an authorized identity and appropriate test data, and nothing
here does.

## The three plan kinds

`create_abac_policy`, `update_abac_policy` and `delete_abac_policy`, registered into the existing
engine so they inherit the whole lifecycle: HMAC binding, TTL, observed-state hash, revalidation,
per-target outcomes, verification, `MODE_READ_ONLY` under `connected_readonly`.

- Validate supported signatures, scopes, privileges, and the documented policy restrictions and
  conflicts, **before** building a plan. A validation failure names what was wrong.
- `delete_abac_policy` widens access by removing a restriction, so it is high impact: require typed
  confirmation, and state in the preview what becomes reachable.
- `update_abac_policy` must not silently overwrite a policy that changed since the preview — that is
  what the observed-state hash is for; make sure the handler observes the policy definition itself,
  not just its name.
- Quote identifiers anywhere a name reaches a statement preview.
